Chapter 2. 
 
Practical Frequency: Creating Frequency Memories 
 
Introduction 
Practical frequency is the applied art of learning, storing, and reproducing frequencies 
within the human body and mind. 
Where theoretical frequency explains how reality works, practical frequency explains how 
humans can interact with it consciously. 
A frequency memory is created when the nervous system, breath, movement, and atten-
tion synchronize with an external rhythm, and then retain that rhythm as an internal refer-
ence. 
This is not imagination. 
It is neurophysiology, entrainment, and embodied learning. 
 
1. What Is a Frequency Memory? 
A frequency memory is: 
• A learned rhythmic pattern 
 
• Stored in the nervous system 
 
• Reproducible without the original external stimulus 
 
Examples of frequency memory: 
• Riding a bicycle 
 
• Walking rhythm 
 
• Musical timing 
 
• Martial arts movement 
 
• Breathing patterns during calm states 
 
Once learned, the body can re-enter the state on command. 

 
 2. Example 1: Ocean Waves (Breath–Body–Mind Synchronization) 
Practice 
1. Listen to ocean waves. 
 
2. Match your breathing to the rise and fall. 
 
3. Add slow, wave-like body movement. 
 
4. Maintain attention on the rhythm for several minutes. 
 
What Happens Scientifically 
• Breathing slows → vagus nerve activation 
 
• Brain shifts toward alpha–theta waves 
 
• Heart rhythm becomes coherent 
 
• Muscular tension releases in cycles 
 
Frequency Memory Created 
The nervous system stores: 
• Tempo 
 
• Depth 
 
• Smoothness 
 
• Predictable rhythm 
 
Later, at home: 
• Breathing alone can recreate the same state 
 
• The body “remembers” the wave 

 
 
3. Example 2: Forest Wind and Trees (Grounding Frequency) 
Practice 
• Stand in a forest or near trees 
 
• Observe how branches sway—not rigid, not collapsing 
 
• Breathe slowly 
 
• Shift weight gently side to side, like the tree responding to wind 
 
Effect 
• Balance systems activate (vestibular system) 
 
• Postural muscles soften 
 
• Nervous system moves from alert to stable 
 
Stored Frequency 
• Stability under movement 
 
• Flexibility without tension 
 
This frequency helps: 
• Anxiety 
 
• Hypervigilance 
 
• Trauma-related body stiffness 
 

 
 
4. Example 3: Flowing Water (Emotional Regulation) 
Practice 
• Watch a river or stream 
 
• Match breathing to continuous flow 
 
• Move arms in circular, uninterrupted patterns 
 
Why It Works 
• The brain prefers continuous patterns 
 
• Circular motion reduces threat perception 
 
• Predictable flow reduces amygdala activity 
 
Healing Effect 
• Emotions move instead of getting stuck 
 
• Reduced rumination 
 
• Increased psychological flexibility 
 
This is why water imagery is used in therapy. 

 
 5. Example 4: Stone and Mountain (Structural Frequency) 
Practice 
• Sit or stand still near a large rock or mountain 
 
• Lengthen the spine 
 
• Slow the breath 
 
• Reduce movement to near-zero 
 
Frequency Copied 
• Extremely low-frequency stability 
 
• Minimal oscillation 
 
• High structural integrity 
 
Neurological Effect 
• Reduced cortical noise 
 
• Lower stress hormones 
 
• Increased sense of safety and presence 
 
This practice supports: 
• Decision-making 
 
• Mental clarity 
 
• Emotional containment 

 
 6. Example 5: Fire (Activation Frequency) 
Practice 
• Watch a flame 
 
• Breathe slightly faster 
 
• Allow sharper, controlled movements 
 
• Maintain awareness without agitation 
 
Stored Frequency 
• Alert but controlled energy 
 
• Precision without panic 
 
Useful for: 
• Performance 
 
• Focus 
 
• Motivation 
 
• Overcoming lethargy 
 
• Fire teaches contained intensity. 

 
 7. Why Practical Frequency Is Healing (Scientific Explanation) 
1. Nervous System Regulation 
The autonomic nervous system learns through repetition and rhythm, not logic. 
• Slow frequencies → parasympathetic dominance (healing mode) 
 
• Chaotic frequencies → stress response 
 
• Coherent rhythms → homeostasis 
 
2. Neuroplasticity 
Repeated rhythmic states: 
• Strengthen neural pathways 
 
• Make calm states more accessible 
 
• Reduce baseline stress over time 
 
The brain learns: 
• “This state is safe. I can return here.” 
• 
 
3. Heart–Brain Coherence 
Studies show that: 
• Rhythmic breathing creates coherent heart rate variability 
 
• Coherence improves emotional regulation 
 
• Cognitive clarity increases 
 
Frequency memory stabilizes this coherence. 
 
4. Trauma and Psychological Healing 
Trauma is often: 
• A disrupted rhythm 
 
• A frozen or chaotic frequency 
 
Practical frequency restores: 
• Predictable cycles 
 
• Safe repetition 
 
• Embodied control 
 
Healing happens through the body, not by force of thought. 

 
 8. Why Nature Is the Best Teacher 
Natural systems: 
• Are self-regulating 
 
• Have stable frequencies 
 
• Have evolved over millions of years 
 
When humans entrain to nature: 
• We borrow stability 
 
• We reduce internal noise 
 
• We remember our original rhythms 
 
Nature does not heal by intention. 
It heals by consistency of frequency. 
 
9. Practical Frequency as Daily Training 
Frequency memory allows you to: 
• Calm yourself without external tools 
 
• Shift emotional state consciously 
 
• Align body and mind before action 
 
• Recover faster from stress 
 
You are no longer dependent on the environment. 
You carry the environment within your nervous system. 
 
Conclusion 
Practical frequency is the art of becoming the rhythm you need. 
When you copy the wave, 
you become fluid. 
When you copy the tree, 
you become stable. 
When you copy the mountain, 
you become unshakable. 
This is not symbolism. 
It is biological resonance, learned and stored. 
Healing happens when the body remembers how harmony feels.
