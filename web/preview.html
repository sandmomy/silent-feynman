    The Field

    Before sound is heard, it is felt. Every room, every body, every breath is already a vibrating instrument — the ear arrives late to a conversation the cells began long before. There is a pressure, a rhythm, a faint reminder held in tissue, and we only name it "hearing" at the last moment of its journey into us.

    This is the first observation of the field: the body reads vibration before the mind translates it into word. A baby hears its mother's voice through bone before it hears it through air. A musician feels the chord before the fingers register the shape. A walker in the forest knows the distant river before the river's sound arrives. The field is listening at scales we rarely invite into language.

    Tune a bell and the bell tunes the room. Tune a room and the room tunes the body. Tune a body and what was noise becomes signal. Resonance is the name for this cascade — not a metaphor, but a description of how matter keeps time with itself when the conditions are right.

    What are the conditions? Space enough for the wave to travel. Material soft enough to receive it. Attention patient enough to notice the return. Every tradition with a contemplative edge has circled this same geometry — the ear of the heart, the silence beneath breath, the small cup inside the skull that fills when nothing is said.

    The chapter opens with a question we carry in our bones: what is the smallest unit of listening? And can it be practiced?
